Marie Curie (a woman from Poland who later became French) was a scientist (a person who studies how the world works) who studied physics (the study of how things move) and chemistry (the study of what things are made of).
In 1903 she got the Nobel Prize (a big prize for science) in physics with her husband Pierre Curie. The reason was their work together on radioactivity (when the atoms of a thing break and give out energy).
In 1911 she got the Nobel Prize in chemistry, because she found 2 new elements (a kind of matter): radium and polonium.
